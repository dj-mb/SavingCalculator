Personal Finance Best Practices

1. Order of Savings Priority

  1. Capture full employer 401(k) match (free 50–100% return)
  2. Pay off high-interest debt (>7% APR)
  3. Build 3–6 months of expenses as an emergency fund (HYSA)
  4. Max HSA on an HDHP (triple tax advantage)
  5. Max Roth or Traditional IRA ($7K/yr; $8K if 50+)
  6. Return to 401(k) and max it ($23,500/yr in 2025)
  7. Fund 529 plans for kids' tuition
  8. Taxable brokerage for anything beyond

2. 50/30/20 Rule

Spend 50% on needs, 30% on wants, save 20%. Aggressive savers target 30–50% saved.

3. Pay Yourself First

Automate transfers to investment accounts on payday so the money never sits in checking.

4. FIRE Math (4% Rule)

Target portfolio = 25× annual expenses. A 4% safe withdrawal rate historically sustains 30+ years.

5. HSA as Stealth Retirement

After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed like a Traditional IRA. Qualified medical withdrawals stay tax-free forever — save receipts and let it grow.

6. 529 Plans

Age-based portfolios shift from stocks to bonds as your child nears college. Many states give a tax deduction for contributions.

7. Asset Allocation Rule of Thumb

% in stocks ≈ 110 − your age. Younger investors ride out volatility; near retirement, shift to bonds for stability.

8. Diversify Cheaply

Low-cost broad-market index funds (total US, total international, total bond) typically beat actively managed funds after fees over long horizons.

9. Dollar-Cost Averaging

Invest a fixed amount on a regular schedule regardless of market level. Removes timing decisions and smooths entry price.

10. Avoid Lifestyle Inflation

When income rises, route the raise into savings before lifestyle adjusts. The largest lever most people have.

11. Roth vs Traditional

Roth if you expect a higher tax bracket in retirement (common when young). Traditional if you expect lower. Many people split.

12. Review Annually

Rebalance allocations, increase contributions with raises, revisit beneficiaries and insurance coverage.

Projections use monthly compounding: FV = PMT × ((1+r)n − 1) / r, where r = annual% / 12, n = years × 12.
